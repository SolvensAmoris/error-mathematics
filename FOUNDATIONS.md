# Foundational Statement

## 1. Research object
This project investigates error, failure, discrepancy, invariance, and transformation as central objects for describing systems and their interactions.

The aim is not merely to improve the accuracy of an existing model. It is to investigate whether the structure of discrepancies can reveal relations that a model focused only on expected or functional behavior does not represent.

## 2. Central principle
**We do not require phenomena to resemble one another. We investigate whether their transformations can be described by common mathematical relations.**

A relation is common only when it can be operationally defined, measured, compared, and shown to remain robust under stated conditions. The generic observation that two systems interact is insufficient.

## 3. Foundational hypothesis
Error may contain structural information that is not visible when inquiry focuses exclusively on expected behavior or successful function.

This is a research hypothesis, not an established universal principle. Some discrepancies will be informative; others will arise from noise, bias, confounding, instrument limitations, data processing, or an ill-posed comparison.

## 4. Symmetry of evaluation
Every hypothesis must admit at least two legitimate outcomes:
- If the proposed relation appears robustly, specify the conditions and domain in which it holds.
- If it does not appear, revise the hypothesis or reject it.

Criteria must be specified before confirmatory analysis wherever feasible. They must not be changed after observing results merely to force acceptance.

## 5. Measurement and observer
The observation process is part of the experimental arrangement. A measured result may depend on the system, interaction, observer, instrument, calibration, sampling procedure, and interpretation model. These contributions may interact and may not be separable by simple addition.

## 6. Initial experimental system
The first proposed laboratory is a water system subjected to controlled mechanical vibration. Its purpose is to characterize how a defined perturbation changes measurable organization, and what remains, disappears, or emerges after the perturbation is removed.

The experiment requires safe apparatus, explicit controls, calibrated measurements, repeated trials, and a protocol written before confirmatory data collection.

## 7. Integrity rules
- A hypothesis is not evidence.
- A simulation is not a physical observation.
- Correlation does not establish causation by itself.
- Absence of a detected effect is not automatically proof of absence.
- A failed experiment may reveal a procedural or measurement limitation; it does not automatically falsify the underlying phenomenon.
- Preserve negative results, deviations, raw data, and analysis decisions.
- Do not claim universality from one system or a small number of examples.

## 8. Purpose
Develop and test mathematical descriptions capable of investigating what remains invariant, what diverges, what fails, and what new relations emerge during interaction and transformation.

The project begins as an open research program. Its mathematical novelty and scientific value must be earned through precise definitions, discriminating predictions, and reproducible tests.
