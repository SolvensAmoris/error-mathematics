# Error Mathematics

Research into error, invariance, failure, observation, and relational transformation.

**Status:** foundational research program. Concepts and hypotheses are provisional.

## Purpose
Investigate whether error, discrepancy, and failure can serve as primary objects of mathematical and scientific inquiry rather than being treated only as defects to eliminate.

The project asks whether transformations in physically different systems can be described through common relational structures without requiring the phenomena themselves to resemble one another.

A second methodological track, **Da Vinci Bridge**, investigates whether independently proven mathematical results can be assembled into valid conclusions that none of the selected results supplies alone.

## Foundational commitments
1. Do not discard a discrepancy before examining its origin, structure, persistence, and dependence on observation conditions.
2. Invariance is a question, not an assumption. State what changes, what remains, and within which limits.
3. Failure is not synonymous with error. A system can fail relative to a requirement; a model can err while the system behaves normally.
4. Observer and instrument are part of measurement conditions and must be characterized.
5. Negative results are evidence. Hypotheses may be delimited, revised, or rejected.
6. Do not force universality. A common mathematical relation must be demonstrated.
7. A mathematical trunk must be independently justified before it is used in an assembly. A valid assembly does not by itself establish novelty.

## Working vocabulary
- Error: a structured difference between specified references, expectations, observations, or model outputs.
- Failure: violation of a specified requirement or loss of a defined capability or relation.
- Invariant: a property or relation preserved under a specified class of transformations.
- Discrepancy: an observed difference whose source or significance has not yet been classified.
- Relational transformation: change in relations among components or systems under specified interaction conditions.
- Mathematical trunk: an independently proven theorem, lemma, identity, principle, or property with explicit conditions.
- Connector: an identity or valid inference that transfers information between mathematical representations.
- Assembly: the arrangement of trunks, constructions, and connectors used to derive a target conclusion.

These are working definitions, not claims that all error or failure has one universal form.

## Initial research tracks
- **MIOD-001 (working label):** organization changes in a water system under controlled vibration. MIOD is provisional and is not an established mathematical theory.
- **Da Vinci Bridge:** mathematical trunk assembly, with pilot cases DB-001 through DB-003.

## Research integrity
A hypothesis is not evidence. A simulation is not a physical observation. Correlation does not by itself establish causation. A measurement is conditional on instrument, calibration, procedure, and model. Preserve raw observations and record exclusions and deviations.

No new universal law has been established. Formal definitions, operational measures, preregistered criteria, and reproducible results are pending. The DB-001 and DB-002 cases reconstruct known theorems; DB-003 records a valid consequence of an established theorem and a counterexample to a stronger conjecture. No mathematical novelty is claimed.
