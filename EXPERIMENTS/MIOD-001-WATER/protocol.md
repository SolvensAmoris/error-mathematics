# MIOD-001 — Water Under Controlled Vibration

**Status:** protocol design draft; not a completed experiment.  
**Version:** 0.1

## 1. Research question
How does a controlled mechanical perturbation change measurable organization in a bounded water system, and which measured relations persist, disappear, or emerge after the perturbation is removed?

## 2. Safety and scope
Use a small, stable container and low-energy vibration apparatus designed for tabletop experiments. Keep electrical equipment isolated from water. Do not use improvised mains-powered equipment near water. Avoid glass containers under mechanical stress. Secure the apparatus against slipping or tipping. Stop if the container, transducer, or support becomes unstable.

This protocol is a design outline, not a substitute for equipment instructions or a risk assessment.

## 3. Variables to define before data collection
Container geometry and material; water volume and temperature; vibration source and mechanical coupling; measured frequency and amplitude at the container or water interface; sensor and camera positions; environmental vibration and airflow; sampling rate, exposure, lighting, and image-processing settings; trial duration and recovery period.

Do not use a loudspeaker volume setting as a proxy for mechanical vibration delivered to the container. Measure the relevant input at or near the system.

## 4. Conditions
At minimum, design:
1. baseline with no applied vibration;
2. sham condition with apparatus present but no applied vibration;
3. controlled vibration at specified settings;
4. perturbation removal and recovery observation.

Randomize trial order where feasible. Repeat conditions. Use a fixed protocol and preserve all trials, including failed or unusable runs with reasons.

## 5. Candidate observables
Choose a small set before confirmatory collection:
- surface displacement over time;
- dominant oscillation frequencies;
- spatial correlation or coherence across selected surface regions;
- persistence and recovery time after perturbation removal;
- repeatability of measured patterns across trials.

A visual pattern alone is insufficient. Specify image calibration, segmentation, and analysis before interpreting results.

## 6. Controls and confounders
Consider ambient vibration, table resonance, container resonance, transducer heating, water temperature, evaporation, air movement, camera motion, lighting variation, and changes in coupling.

## 7. Analysis plan
Separate exploratory from confirmatory analysis. Define the primary outcome, exclusion criteria, uncertainty estimation, baseline comparison, competing models, and acceptance, limitation, and rejection criteria.

Do not select a preferred frequency or metric after inspecting results and then report it as a preregistered prediction.

## 8. Data and provenance
For each run, record a unique trial ID, date/time, operator, apparatus identifiers, calibration, condition, input measurements, environmental conditions, raw-file hashes, deviations, and analysis version.

Keep raw recordings immutable. Store derived data and code separately.

## 9. Current status
No physical data have been collected or analyzed under this protocol. Variables and acceptance criteria remain to be finalized before confirmatory trials.
