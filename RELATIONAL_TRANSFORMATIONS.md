# Relational Transformations

**Status:** provisional notation and research program.

## 1. Motivation
The project investigates whether a useful mathematical description can begin with changes in relations among systems rather than assuming that each system must first be represented by a fixed object or shape.

This is not a rejection of existing mathematics. Existing tools may be used as instruments, baselines, and controls. The question is whether a new formal structure is needed to represent aspects they do not capture adequately.

## 2. Minimal interaction description
(A_state, B_state, coupling, conditions) -> observed relational transformation

A_state is the characterized state of the perturbing or interacting system. B_state is the characterized state of the receiving or coupled system. Coupling describes operationally how systems influence one another. Conditions include relevant environmental and measurement conditions. The relational transformation is a measured change in specified relations.

The labels A and B do not imply that all interactions are one-way. Reciprocal coupling may require a different representation.

## 3. Candidate operator
T(A, B, C) -> Delta_R

T is a placeholder for a transformation rule; Delta_R denotes a measured change in a declared relational description. Neither is yet a defined mathematical operator.

## 4. Required formal work
- Define what counts as a system and its boundary.
- Define state descriptors without assuming they are complete.
- Define coupling in domain-specific operational terms.
- Specify how relations are represented and compared.
- Distinguish direct effects, indirect effects, and measurement effects.
- Determine whether a common operator can be mapped across domains without losing discriminating power.

## 5. Candidate questions
Which relations persist under perturbation? Which break, and under what conditions? Which emerge only after interaction? Does the receiving system return to its initial organization after perturbation removal? Can different systems share a transformation rule despite different physical observables?

## 6. Caution
“Interaction changes systems” is too general to function as a scientific law. The research must identify a nontrivial, measurable relation that makes predictions and can fail.
